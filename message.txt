The biggest misconception in retail algorithmic trading is that hedge funds have "secret indicators."

They don't.

What firms like **Citadel**, **Two Sigma**, **Renaissance Technologies**, **Jane Street**, and **DE Shaw** do is engineer **thousands of features** from many data sources and combine them with rigorous validation, risk management, and execution. Most of their edge comes from **feature engineering, data quality, execution, and research**, not a magic RSI setting.

Below is a comprehensive list of features you can engineer from your OHLCV data.

# A practical feature set

For your gold M5/H1 project, a realistic and strong starting point would be around **300–800 engineered features**, including:

* Multiple moving averages and their slopes.
* Momentum and volatility indicators across several window lengths.
* Candlestick geometry and pattern flags.
* Rolling statistics and lagged values.
* Multi-timeframe features (H1 and H4 mapped onto M5).
* Session and calendar information.
* Support/resistance distance measures.
* Market regime classification.
* Cross-asset context if you can obtain those datasets.

This is already a substantial feature space for models like XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, or neural networks, while remaining manageable for experimentation. The key is not to include every possible feature indiscriminately, but to validate which ones improve out-of-sample trading performance and avoid overfitting.
